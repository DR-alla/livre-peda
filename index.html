Learning Objectives

By the end of this section, students will be able to:

  1. Distinguish the formal-organic criterion from the material-objective criterion.
  2. Explain the broad and narrow meanings of administrative law.
  3. Compare the legal approaches described for Anglo-Saxon and Latin systems.
  4. Identify the main fields covered by administrative law in Algeria under the narrow meaning.

Key Terminology

المعيار العضوي أو الشكليFormal-organic criterionDefines administrative law by reference to the organization and structure of administrative bodies and public legal persons.
المعيار المادي أو الموضوعي أو الوظيفيMaterial-objective-functional criterionDefines administrative law by reference to the functions and activities performed by administrative bodies to serve the public interest.

المعنى الواسعBroad senseAll legal rules, whatever their source, that govern public administration, including its organization, activity, property, and disputes.
المعنى الضيقNarrow senseThe distinct rules, different from private law, that apply to public administration when it exercises administrative functions.

امتيازات السلطة العامةPublic-authority privilegesLegal powers available to public administrative bodies in pursuit of the public interest, including unilateral decisions that affect individuals’ legal positions.

Definition of Administrative Law
A. By criterion
Formal-organic criterion
  • Focus: the external organization of administration.
  • Covers administrative bodies, structures, and public legal persons.
  • Examples in the source include wilayas, municipalities, universities, and hospitals.
Material-objective-functional criterion
  • Focus: the substance of administrative functions and activities.
  • Covers activities and services performed to meet public needs and serve the public interest.
Modern mixed definition
  • Combines both approaches.
  • Administrative law organizes administrative bodies and governs their activities in pursuit of the public interest.
B. By scope
Broad sense
  • Includes all legal rules governing public administration, whatever their source.
  • Covers organization, activity, property, relations with users, and resulting disputes.
  • In the Anglo-Saxon approach described in the source:
    • Administration is subject to the same rules as individuals and private bodies.
    • Disputes are heard under the same rules and procedures before one judicial authority.
    • Unity of law is presented as a safeguard against administrative abuse.
Narrow sense
  • Includes rules distinct from private law and specifically applicable to administration.
  • Is described as exceptional law because it departs from ordinary private-law rules.
  • Is associated in the source with Latin systems, particularly France, and a dual judicial system.
  • Administration may be subject to two sets of rules:
    • Private law: when it acts at the level of individuals; disputes fall within the ordinary courts.
    • Administrative law: when it acts in pursuit of the public interest using public-authority privileges, including unilateral decisions.
C. Consequences of the narrow sense for administrative organization in Algeria
Organization of administrative authority
  • Administrative bodies and units, their relationships, and central or decentralized structures.
Administrative activity
  • Administrative police: preventive maintenance of public order.
  • Public services: meeting public needs through services and goods, with regular and efficient operation.
Methods of administration
  • Human and material means: including public employees and public property.
  • Legal means: administrative acts, including unilateral administrative decisions and administrative contracts made with another party’s agreement.
Administrative disputes
  • The competent judicial authority and the applicable procedural aspects.
